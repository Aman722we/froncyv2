Yes. I think you now have enough to **monetize Froncy V2 without building another big feature**.

The important shift is this:

> **Froncy is not selling “job alerts.” Froncy is selling earlier access to relevant opportunities + a faster path from job → human → application.**

That is much easier to monetize.

Current products are already charging for combinations of early job access, HR/recruiter contacts, resume tools, and career help: MentorSetu publicly lists ₹119/month or ₹299/3 months with verified jobs, HR contacts and one-time resume review; Carrerlift lists ₹99/month and ₹249/3 months with early jobs, HR contacts and resume tools; another Telegram job service charges ₹299 lifetime for early alerts and HR contacts. ([Telegram][1])

So I would **not invent a complicated SaaS pricing model**.

## What I would launch today

### Free

Keep the core job discovery free.

A user gets:

* Profile-based job matching
* Career-page jobs
* Legitimate source
* Basic job details
* Normal notifications

This is your acquisition engine.

### Froncy Pro — ₹199/month

Give Pro three things:

**1. Instant job alerts**

They don't wait for a digest. When a relevant job appears from one of your monitored career pages, they get it immediately.

The selling point isn't:

> “We have 60 career pages.”

It's:

> **“Know about relevant jobs as soon as they appear on the company's career page.”**

And be careful with “less competition.” You don't currently have evidence that these jobs have fewer applicants. Say **“direct-source / early access”** rather than claiming low competition.

**2. HR Contact**

Your button idea is very good.

Job appears:

> **[Apply Now] [Find HR Contact]**

User clicks **Find HR Contact**.

You manually research the appropriate public professional contact and attach it to that job.

Initially, I'd limit this to **5 contact requests/month** for Pro.

That protects your time.

And only provide legitimate professional/public contact information—not private/personal data.

**3. One-time Resume Review**

Yes, I would add it now.

But **don't turn Froncy into a resume-consulting business.**

Make it a constrained benefit:

> **1 human resume review every 3 months**

or perhaps one review during the first month for early Pro users.

The user already demonstrated the demand themselves by coming back to you and asking:

> “Can you see my resume and tell me why I am not getting interview calls?”

That's exactly the kind of high-intent request that can convert.

# But there is one thing I'd change in your thinking

You said:

> “career pages + direct jobs + HR contacts + resume review”

I wouldn't consider those four equal products.

There is a hierarchy:

**Career-page monitoring = acquisition**

↓

**Relevant instant alert = core product**

↓

**HR contact = monetization / execution**

↓

**Resume review = high-intent human service**

That's a very clean business.

The job alert gets the user into Froncy.

The contact helps them **act**.

The resume review helps them understand **why they're not converting**.

That creates a logical loop:

**Opportunity → Apply → Contact → Resume diagnosis → Apply better → Repeat**

That is much stronger than:

> “Here's another AI cover-letter generator.”

---

# And I would NOT charge for all job alerts yet

This is important.

Your current biggest unknown isn't:

> “Will people pay ₹199?”

It's:

> **“Will people pay specifically for the Froncy workflow?”**

So don't put everything behind a paywall immediately.

Run a very small monetization experiment.

Suppose you currently have ~200 users.

Put Pro in front of them **today**.

Your first goal isn't ₹1 lakh.

Your first goal is:

> **Get 5 people to actually pay.**

Five people × ₹199 = ₹995.

The money isn't the important part.

The signal is.

If 5 people pay without you personally convincing each one, you have something.

Then try 20.

Then 50.

# Even better: sell the outcome, not the features

Nobody cares about Greenhouse.

Say:

That's the product.
